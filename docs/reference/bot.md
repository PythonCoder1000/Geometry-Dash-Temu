# The honest state of Geometry Dash bots on macOS
> Archived research, not current setup instructions for this project.

**No AI has ever beaten a Hard Demon, let alone an Extreme Demon — every "bot completion" of Tidal Wave, Acheron, Slaughterhouse or Silent Clubstep you have ever seen is a human-recorded macro played back frame-perfectly.** That is the single most important fact for a Mac user evaluating "GD bots" in 2026. The practical question is therefore not "which AI should I use" but "which macro/replay mod actually has a working macOS build through Geode, and which auto-generates macros so I don't have to record runs myself." The winning stack is **Geode + Pathfinder (auto-generates the macro) + GDH or Prism Menu's Quartz Bot (plays it back) + Click Between Frames (input precision) + ZCB Live (optional click sounds)**. Crucially, the two bots most often recommended online — **xdBot** and **MegaHack** — do not run on macOS at all, and several "Mac MegaHack" downloads circulating on itch.io are reskinned malware. Using any of these tools on rated levels invalidates Pointercrate/AREDL records and can earn a shadow-ban from RobTop's global leaderboard.

## What "GD bot" actually means in 2026

The community uses the word "bot" for at least three completely different things, and conflating them is how people end up downloading the wrong tool. A **macro/replay bot** (xdBot, GDH, MegaHack, Silicate, zBot, ToastyReplay) hooks into Geometry Dash's input pipeline, records each press to a specific physics tick or frame, and then re-injects those inputs deterministically. A **clicksound bot** (ZCB, Click Sounds Full) reads the same macro file but only produces audio — it never touches the game, and it's what makes a botted run sound like a real mechanical keyboard on YouTube. A **true AI agent** (DQN/PPO/NEAT projects on GitHub) tries to learn to play through reinforcement learning or imitation; in practice it is a research toy that maxes out at beating Stereo Madness. **Almost every "AI plays Geometry Dash" video is mislabeled clickbait** — the gameplay is a macro and the "AI" branding is rhetorical.

The autonomous-solving capability people imagine when they say "AI bot" actually exists, but not as a neural network. It exists as **camila314's Pathfinder**, a Geode mod that runs GD's physics inside a simulator and brute-forces a sequence of jump frames that survives the level, then exports it as a `.gdr` macro. It is not learned and not adaptive — it's an algorithmic search over the deterministic physics engine — but it is the closest thing to a "press button → level solved" tool that exists today. **Pathfinder is what's behind the recent flood of effortless extreme-demon clears by people who never touched the level.**

## Why true AI agents fail at Geometry Dash

Every serious attempt — from the Stanford CS231N 2017 paper (Li & Rafferty's DQN with prioritized experience replay) through MCJack123's DashBot 3.0 (genetic algorithm, beat Stereo Madness on stream, **explicitly broken on GD 2.2**) to the current state-of-the-art **ThePickleGawd geometry-dash-ai** project (Mixture-of-Experts DQN with a custom Geode-based Gymnasium environment, Stanford-affiliated team, **beats Stereo Madness only**) — runs into the same wall. Demons require **thousands of frame-perfect inputs in a row**, the reward signal is sparse and only arrives at the end, the agent cannot pause to think, and visually adjacent frames demand opposite actions. Tidal Wave alone has hundreds of frame-perfect timings across two minutes; even a 99.9%-per-input agent fails effectively 100% of the time. Add wildly variable user-made art (particles, shaders, color cycling) plus seven game modes with different physics, and the per-level training cost becomes astronomical with no save states or parallel envs. The 2017 Stanford paper is admirably honest: their agent **never completed any level**.

Two RL projects do target macOS specifically. **ThePickleGawd's geometry-dash-ai** (github.com/ThePickleGawd/geometry-dash-ai, last release Sep 30 2025) is Mac-first, builds on Geode SDK 4.4, and is the most credible academic-grade RL effort against real GD 2.2. **KJ14GOD's GeometryDashAgent** (MIT-licensed, macOS 14+ tested, YOLO + PPO + behavior cloning bootstrap) is also Mac-native but ships no pretrained weights. Both are explicitly research tools whose ceiling is Stereo Madness. **If your goal is to actually clear hard levels, ignore the AI category entirely.**

## Macro replayer compatibility on macOS

The Geode mod loader itself is solid on Mac — it ships a universal binary supporting both Intel x86_64 and Apple Silicon arm64 down to the TulipHook layer, requires macOS 14 Sonoma or newer, and targets the Steam build of GD 2.2074/2.2081. **The Mac App Store version of Geometry Dash is sandboxed and cannot be modded** under any circumstances; this is the single biggest gotcha for new Mac modders. Once Geode is in place, the question becomes which specific replay mod has a Mac binary in its package. The answer is narrower than most online tutorials suggest:

The two tools the user's brief specifically asked about — **xdBot and MegaHack — are both off the table on Mac**. xdBot's mod.json explicitly omits the `mac` and `ios` keys, the GitHub repo was archived by Zilko in June 2025 with v2.4.0 as the last release, and any "Mac fork" you find should be treated with suspicion until you've inspected its mod.json yourself. MegaHack has been Windows-only for its entire history; the "Mac MegaHack" download on dycestanley3's itch.io page is **not endorsed by Absolute and is almost certainly malware**.

## How macro replay actually beats extreme demons

Modern replay mods do not use OS-level autoclickers. An AppleScript or Hammerspoon `eventtap` macro that fires `space` keypresses at fixed intervals is **useless on hard levels** because GD physics drift relative to wall-clock time, and a single frame off equals death. Real macro mods hook PlayLayer's input handler and store inputs against either the in-game frame number, the player's X-position, or the physics tick (240 TPS in 2.2 by default). On playback they re-inject inputs at the exact same engine state. The mainstream open format is **GDR/GDR2** (maxnut/GDReplayFormat), supported by xdBot, GDH's ReplayEngine, MegaHack's `.mhr`, Prism's Quartz Bot, Eclipse, and ZCB's reader. Silicate uses its own faster `.slc` format. **Once you have a `.gdr` file for a level, any of these mods can play it.**

Where do `.gdr` files come from? Three sources. The first is **human frame-by-frame TAS construction** using xdBot or MegaHack's frame stepper plus show-trajectory plus speedhack — this is how the original Tidal Wave, Acheron, and Slaughterhouse macros circulating on github.com/iZySCRIPT-rebeellion/XDBot-Macros and the MacroSharing Discord were built. The second is **community macro libraries**: download a `.gdr` someone else made and load it. The third — and the one that makes Pathfinder genuinely revolutionary — is **algorithmic macro generation**: Pathfinder simulates GD's physics, brute-forces jump-frame sequences that survive each segment (handling cube/ship/ball/UFO/wave/robot/spider/swing modes plus dash orbs and teleport portals), and exports a `.gdr` ready to play. Slopes and some triggers remain partially supported, so a few highly decorated levels won't clean-simulate, but most extreme demons are tractable.

## What I recommend for a Mac user

The single best stack as of May 2026 has four parts. Install **Geode for macOS** from geode-sdk.org/install (Steam build only, requires Sonoma+). Add **GDH** as your mod menu and macro player — it's actively maintained by TobyAdd, MIT-licensed, declares Mac support directly in its mod.json, and includes ReplayEngine v3 plus auto-clicker, frame stepper, hitboxes, FFmpeg renderer, and a built-in macro editor. Add **Pathfinder** for autonomous macro generation; it's the only practical "AI-like" tool that actually clears hard levels on demand. Add **Click Between Frames** for legitimate input precision in any manual practice you do — it was officially adopted by RobTop as "Click Between Steps" (CBS) in patch 2.208 on January 27 2026, making it the only bot-adjacent mod that's **leaderboard-legal**. If you want satisfying clicking sounds for showcase videos, add **ZCB Live**. **Prism Menu with Quartz Bot is an equally valid replacement for GDH** if you prefer its UI; both read GDR macros.

To get this working concretely on a Mac: quit GD and Steam, download `geode-installer-vX.X.X-mac.pkg` from geode-sdk.org/install, **right-click → Open** to bypass Gatekeeper (or run `xattr -cr ~/Downloads/geode-installer-*.pkg`), let it auto-detect `~/Library/Application Support/Steam/steamapps/common/Geometry Dash/Geometry Dash.app`, then launch GD via Steam. A new Geode button appears at the bottom of the main menu. Click it, go to the Download tab, search "GDH" → install, then "Pathfinder" → install, then "Click Between Frames" → install, then optionally "ZCB Live" → install. Restart GD. To bot a level, open Pathfinder, point it at the level, let it generate the macro, then load that `.gdr` into GDH's ReplayEngine and hit play. **If you ever update Steam's GD version, re-run the Geode installer** — Apple's code-signing will otherwise refuse to launch the patched binary. Your save lives at `~/Library/Application Support/GeometryDash/CCGameManager.dat`; back it up before installing anything.

## The detection and ethics reality

Geometry Dash itself has effectively no real-time anti-cheat — no BattlEye, no EAC, no memory integrity check. RobTop's server does flag implausible stat injections and silently removes accounts from the Global Leaderboard (the "shadow leaderboard ban"), and submitted verifications for level rating get manually reviewed against the saved attempt data, which is how Riot's Bloodbath verification was eventually invalidated. The real enforcement layer is **Pointercrate's Demonlist team**, which requires raw unedited footage at ≥30 FPS, an isolated click-audio track ("2TA"), a visible FPS counter, handcam on 2-player levels, and Mega Hack's Cheat Indicator visible if used. They detect macros through frame-identical inputs across multiple attempts, void clicks (silent triggered presses), watermark forensics, and cursor drift analysis. The 2023 SpaceUK scandal — the then-#1 Pointercrate player confessed to botting **every Main List completion since May 2022** using zBot — established the modern raw-footage standards; he was caught partly because zBot's "Level Complete" graphic was 940 pixels wide versus the vanilla 1004. **Submitting any macro-assisted run to Pointercrate, AREDL, or RobTop's leaderboards is a permanent stats wipe**, and refusing to provide raw footage on request is itself bannable. Click Between Frames is the lone exception that's been formally legitimized.

## Conclusion: rethink the question

The framing "what's the best AI bot for Mac" leads to the wrong answer because it presupposes a category of tool that, for practical purposes, does not exist for hard content. The right framing is "what's the best deterministic macro stack for Mac" — and the answer is **Geode + GDH + Pathfinder + CBF**, with ZCB Live optional for video production. This stack gives you exactly the capability the question was reaching for: point it at an extreme demon, get a working completion, with full Apple Silicon support and active 2026 maintenance. The genuinely interesting research direction — model-based RL with a learned simulator that could generalize across visually wild user levels — is being done by nobody publicly, and would still need years before approaching what Pathfinder's algorithmic search delivers today. Meanwhile, the two bots Mac users keep being told to install — xdBot and MegaHack — are unavailable on the platform, and the surrounding "Mac MegaHack" ecosystem is mostly malware. **Use the right Mac-native stack, treat it strictly as single-player entertainment, and never submit a botted run anywhere with a leaderboard attached.**
